# Mediterranean Fisherman's Soup

There's a particular magic that happens when saffron meets fish in a hot broth—the threads release their golden pigments, perfuming the liquid with that unmistakable honey-floral note that defines the coastal cuisines of Provence and Catalonia. This soup celebrates that union, building layers of sweetness from slow-caramelised fennel and leeks, a splash of dry white wine, and just enough tomato paste to give the broth backbone without overwhelming the delicate fish. The addition of salmon provides richness and omega-3s while keeping the dish firmly in high-protein territory. Serve in warmed bowls to maintain temperature throughout the meal—this is a dish best enjoyed immediately, the fish silky and the broth deeply aromatic.

## Ingredients

- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 medium fennel bulb, thinly sliced (keep fronds for garnish)
- 1 medium leek, thinly sliced
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 medium carrot, diced
- 1 red bell pepper, diced
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- ¼ tsp saffron threads
- 250ml dry white wine
- 500ml low-sodium fish or vegetable stock
- 1 bay leaf
- 300g skinless firm white fish fillets (cod or hake), cut into 5cm pieces
- 150g skinless salmon fillet, cut into 3cm pieces
- Zest of ½ lemon
- Fresh parsley, roughly chopped

## Instructions

1. Heat olive oil in a large heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat. Add sliced fennel and leek with a pinch of salt. Cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and lightly golden, about 8 minutes—you want deep caramelization on the edges, not pale steaming.
2. Add garlic, carrot, and diced pepper. Stir and cook 3 minutes until fragrant. Create a small well in the centre, add tomato paste, and cook 90 seconds until it darkens to a deep brick-red colour.
3. Crumble saffron threads into the wine and swirl to bloom. Pour into the pot along with fish stock and add bay leaf. Bring to a vigorous boil, then reduce heat and simmer 10 minutes until vegetables are tender and broth develops depth.
4. Season fish pieces lightly with salt. Add white fish first, submerging it into the broth. Poach 3 minutes, then nestle salmon pieces alongside. Continue cooking 4-5 minutes until fish flakes easily when pressed with a fork—do not stir, which will break the pieces apart.
5. Remove bay leaf. Finish with lemon zest, a generous squeeze of juice, and a drizzle of excellent olive oil. Taste and adjust seasoning. Ladle into warmed bowls, garnish with reserved fennel fronds and chopped parsley. Serve immediately with crusty wholegrain bread if desired.

## Chef's Tips

For the most vibrant saffron colour and flavour, always bloom the threads in warm liquid (wine works beautifully) for at least 5 minutes before adding to the pot. The fish selection matters less than the cut—ensure uniform 5cm pieces so everything cooks evenly. If using frozen fish, thaw completely and pat dry before poaching. This soup keeps refrigerated for 24 hours, but the fish will continue to absorb liquid and soften; for meal prep, consider cooking the base broth and vegetables, then adding fresh fish when reheating individual portions. Leftover broth without fish freezes perfectly for up to 3 months—add the fish directly from frozen when reheating.
