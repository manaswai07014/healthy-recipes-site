# Spiced Chickpea & Couscous Bowls with Tangy Tahini Drizzle

These satisfying bowls bring together the nutty warmth of whole wheat couscous with caramelized chickpeas that develop a crispy exterior when seared in a hot pan. The key is allowing the cumin-spiced chickpeas to develop color—this Maillard reaction creates depth and complexity that elevates the dish beyond simple weeknight cooking. A silky tahini sauce, brightened with lemon and tempered with water, pools around the vegetables and grains, creating contrasting textures with every bite. The raw cucumber and halved tomatoes provide a refreshing counterpoint to the warm couscous, making this a complete meal that feels substantial without weighing you down. Perfect for meal prep when you keep each component separate until serving time.

## Ingredients

- 1 can chickpeas (drained and rinsed)
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
- 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1 cup whole wheat couscous
- 2 tablespoons tahini
- 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice
- 1 small garlic clove, minced
- 3-4 tablespoons warm water
- 2 tablespoons finely chopped parsley
- 1 small cucumber, diced
- 1 pint cherry tomatoes, halved
- 6 cups mixed salad greens

## Instructions

1. Drain and rinse the chickpeas thoroughly. Pat dry with paper towels for better spice adhesion. In a medium bowl, toss the chickpeas with 1 teaspoon of the olive oil, ground cumin, and kosher salt until evenly coated. Set aside to marinate while you prepare the other components.
2. Bring 1 1/4 cups water to a rolling boil in a medium saucepan with a tight-fitting lid. Add the couscous and 1/4 teaspoon kosher salt. Stir once, then immediately remove from heat. Cover and let stand for exactly 5 minutes—the grains will absorb all the liquid and become tender without any further effort. Fluff with a fork and stir in the remaining 2 teaspoons olive oil, another 1/4 teaspoon salt, and the chopped parsley. Cover loosely to keep warm.
3. While the couscous rests, make the tahini drizzle: In a small bowl, whisk together the tahini, lemon juice, and minced garlic until smooth. Slowly add warm water, one tablespoon at a time, whisking constantly until you reach a pourable consistency similar to heavy cream. The sauce should coat a spoon with visible ribbons.
4. Heat a large skillet over medium-high heat. Add the spiced chickpeas and cook for 4-5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until they're warmed through and develop some golden-brown spots on the outside. You should hear a gentle sizzle—this indicates proper browning without steaming.
5. Prepare the vegetables: Peel and dice the cucumber into 1/2-inch pieces. Slice the cherry tomatoes in half lengthwise. Divide the salad greens between two large shallow bowls, creating a fluffy bed.
6. Spoon the warm couscous over the greens, then arrange the seared chickpeas, cucumber, and tomatoes in distinct sections. Drizzle 2 tablespoons of the tahini sauce over each bowl in a zigzag pattern. Serve immediately while the couscous is still warm and the vegetables retain their fresh crunch.

## Chef's Tips

The tahini sauce is the flexible component here—adjust the water amount based on your preference for drizzling versus coating consistency. If your tahini is particularly thick, you may need an extra tablespoon of water. For added depth, toast the cumin in the dry skillet for 30 seconds before cooking the chickpeas—this blooms the spices and intensifies their flavor. Leftovers store beautifully: keep the couscous, chickpeas, vegetables, and tahini sauce in separate airtight containers in the refrigerator for up to 4 days. When ready to serve, gently reheat the couscous and chickpeas together, then assemble fresh bowls with the raw vegetables and sauce. You can also meal prep the entire bowl, but add the tahini dressing just before serving to prevent the greens from wilting.
