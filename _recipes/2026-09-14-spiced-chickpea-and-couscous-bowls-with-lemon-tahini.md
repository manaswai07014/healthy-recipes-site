# Spiced Chickpea and Couscous Bowls with Lemon Tahini

These buildable bowls bring together the warm, earthy tones of spiced chickpeas with the light, fluffy texture of whole wheat couscous. The chickpeas develop a satisfying crunch on the outside while remaining creamy within, their flavor deepened by cumin and a whisper of smoked paprika. Cool cucumber and bright cherry tomatoes cut through the richness, while a silky tahini sauce—brightened with lemon and garlic—ties every element together. A small dollop of Greek yogurt adds a cooling tang that rounds out the plate. This is the kind of meal that feels substantial without weighing you down: ready in 30 minutes, packed with 19 grams of protein, and endlessly adaptable to whatever vegetables you have on hand.

## Ingredients

- 1 can chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 tablespoon olive oil
- 1 teaspoon cumin
- 1/2 teaspoon smoked paprika
- 1 cup whole wheat couscous
- 1 1/4 cups water
- 3/4 teaspoon kosher salt
- 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
- 2 tablespoons tahini
- 1 small garlic clove, grated
- 2 tablespoons fresh parsley, minced
- 1 small cucumber, diced
- 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
- 4 cups mixed salad greens
- 2 tablespoons Greek yogurt

## Instructions

1. Drain and rinse chickpeas, then pat very dry with paper towels. This step is non-negotiable—surface moisture prevents proper caramelization.
2. Toss dried chickpeas with 1/2 tablespoon olive oil, cumin, smoked paprika, and 1/4 teaspoon salt in a mixing bowl until evenly coated.
3. Heat remaining 1/2 tablespoon oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Add chickpeas in a single layer and cook undisturbed for 3-4 minutes until bottoms are golden and blistered. Give the pan a good shake, cook 2 minutes more, then remove from heat.
4. Bring 1 1/4 cups water and 1/4 teaspoon salt to a rolling boil in a medium saucepan. Pour in couscous, stir once, cover tightly, and remove from heat. Let steam for exactly 5 minutes—the grains should be tender with no chalky centers.
5. Whisk together lemon juice, tahini, grated garlic, and 2-3 tablespoons cold water until a smooth, pourable consistency forms. Season with remaining salt.
6. Fluff couscous with a fork, then fold in parsley and a drizzle of olive oil. The residual heat will bloom the herbs without cooking away their brightness.
7. Build bowls by placing greens as the base, then mound couscous off-center. Arrange spiced chickpeas, cucumber, and tomatoes in distinct sections for visual appeal. Add a dollop of Greek yogurt and finish with a generous drizzle of tahini sauce. Serve immediately while the chickpeas retain their crisp edges.

## Chef's Tips

For the crispiest chickpeas, ensure they're completely dry before hitting the pan—a single paper towel session prevents steaming and promotes proper browning. If you want to meal prep these bowls, store each component separately and assemble just before serving; the couscous will dry out and the greens will wilt if left dressed overnight. The tahini sauce can be made up to 5 days ahead and stored in an airtight container in the refrigerator; it may thicken as it sits, so whisk in a tablespoon of warm water before using. For a flavor variation, try substituting the smoked paprika with sumac for a more traditional lemony Middle Eastern profile, or add a pinch of cayenne if you prefer heat.
