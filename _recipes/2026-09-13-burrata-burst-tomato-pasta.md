# Burrata & Burst Tomato Pasta

There's something undeniably theatrical about tearing open a ball of burrata at the dinner table. Its milky, yielding interior spills across pasta dressed in a deeply flavored tomato sauce, creating moments of pure indulgence with every bite. This recipe channels the spirit of a trattoria in Modena—where simple ingredients meet technique and restraint. The sauce builds flavor through patient reduction, concentrating the tomatoes until they become jammy and complex. Fresh cherry tomatoes add bright sweetness that cuts through the richness, while a generous handful of basil brings that unmistakable Italian herbal note. The result is a restaurant-worthy dish that comes together in under 40 minutes, proving that sometimes the most memorable meals are built on restraint rather than excess.

## Ingredients

- 4 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, divided
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 400g can whole San Marzano tomatoes
- 1 tsp fine sea salt
- Large handful fresh basil, roughly torn
- 225g spaghetti or bucatini
- 150g cherry tomatoes, halved
- 30g Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
- 1 ball fresh burrata (115g)

## Instructions

1. Place a wide saucepan over medium heat. Add 2 tablespoons of the olive oil, then drop in the sliced garlic. Cook, stirring frequently, for 45 to 60 seconds until the edges turn golden and the kitchen fills with that sweet, toasty aroma. Watch closely—the garlic should be fragrant but not brown.
2. Dump in the canned tomatoes with their liquid (the pan will hiss and steam, so stand back). Use a sturdy wooden spoon to crush the tomatoes into rough, irregular chunks—no need for perfection here. Stir in the salt and three-quarters of the torn basil. Let the sauce come to an active simmer, then partially cover and cook 10 to 12 minutes until it thickens and the bright red color deepens to a richer hue. The surface should bubble steadily but not aggressively.
3. While the sauce reduces, bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil. Salt it until it tastes pleasantly seasoned—not seawater, but noticeably briny. Cook the pasta 2 minutes shy of package directions; it should offer gentle resistance when bitten. Reserve 120ml of the starchy pasta water before draining.
4. Swirl the drained pasta into the tomato sauce. Add a splash of the reserved pasta water and toss vigorously, letting the starches meld with the sauce. The noodles should be glossy and well-coated, with the sauce clinging to every strand. Remove from heat and fold in the remaining torn basil.
5. Divide between warmed shallow bowls. Scatter the halved cherry tomatoes around the perimeter. Shower with the grated Parmigiano, then place the burrata ball in the center. Drizzle with the remaining 2 tablespoons of olive oil and finish with a few fresh basil leaves. Serve immediately, instructing guests to tear the burrata at the table and fold it through the warm pasta—the cream inside will melt into a luxurious sauce.

## Chef's Tips

For the most dramatic burrata moment, bring the cheese to room temperature for 20 minutes before serving—this ensures the creamy center flows when torn. If cherry tomatoes are out of season, skip them entirely rather than using flavorless ones; the sauce stands on its own. To make this even lighter, substitute half the spaghetti with zucchini noodles tossed in during the last minute of cooking. Leftovers reheat well, though the burrata is best added fresh to each serving; store it separately and tear it in when plating. The sauce can be made up to three days ahead and refrigerated, developing even deeper flavor overnight.
