# Rosemary Lamb Fillet with Sicilian Caponata

This dish takes the sun-soaked flavors of Sicily and gives them a lean, weeknight-friendly spin. The caponata is the star here: aubergine and green pepper melt down into a glossy, sweet-and-sour stew, lifted by briny olives, capers, and a whisper of balsamic. It's the kind of thing that tastes even better the next day, but we're not waiting. Against that tangy backdrop, a rosemary-rubbed lamb fillet roasts to rosy perfection, its juices mingling with the vegetables. Baby potatoes crisp at the edges, and a bed of wilted spinach rounds things out with a hit of iron and green. It's a plate that feels generous but stays light, clocking in under 500 calories with a solid 40 grams of protein. Whether you're feeding a small household or meal-prepping for the week, this one earns its spot at the table.

## Ingredients

- 3 garlic cloves (2 sliced, 1 grated)
- 2 tsp rapeseed oil, for the caponata
- 1 small red onion, cut into wedges
- 1 medium aubergine, sliced and quartered
- 200 g passata
- 1 green bell pepper, deseeded and sliced
- 6 pitted Kalamata olives, halved and rinsed
- 2 tsp capers, rinsed
- 2 tsp fresh rosemary, finely chopped
- 1 tsp balsamic vinegar
- 4 baby new potatoes, halved
- 1 tsp rapeseed oil, for the lamb
- 250 g lean lamb loin fillet, trimmed of visible fat
- 240 g baby spinach
- 1 tbsp fresh parsley, finely chopped (optional)

## Instructions

1. Step 1: Prepare the caponata base. Thinly slice two garlic cloves; finely grate the third and set aside for the lamb. Warm 2 tsp rapeseed oil in a wide, heavy pan over medium heat. Add the onion wedges and cook for 5 minutes until softened and translucent.
2. Step 2: Build the caponata. Stir in the aubergine and cook for 5 minutes more, until it begins to collapse and take on color. Pour in the passata, then add the green pepper, olives, capers, rosemary, and balsamic vinegar. Cover and simmer gently for 15 minutes, stirring often, until the vegetables are tender and the sauce has thickened.
3. Step 3: Roast the lamb and potatoes. Heat the oven to 190°C (170°C fan). Boil the potatoes for 10 minutes, then drain. Combine the grated garlic, remaining rosemary, and a generous grind of black pepper, then rub this mixture all over the lamb. Toss the potatoes with 1 tsp oil and more pepper. Place the lamb and potatoes in a small roasting tin and roast for 15–20 minutes, until the lamb is cooked to your liking.
4. Step 4: Wilt the spinach. While the lamb roasts, wilt the spinach in a covered pan over medium heat or in the microwave, then squeeze out any excess liquid.
5. Step 5: Finish and serve. Stir the reserved sliced garlic into the caponata and cook for 1 minute more. Rest the lamb for 5 minutes, then slice. Serve over the caponata with the potatoes and spinach, scattered with parsley if using.

## Chef's Tips

For a vegetarian version, omit the lamb and fold a drained can of chickpeas into the caponata during the last 5 minutes of simmering; you'll still hit around 18g protein per serving. The caponata itself is highly adaptable: swap the green pepper for roasted red peppers, or add a pinch of chilli flakes for heat. It keeps beautifully in the fridge for up to 4 days, and the flavors deepen overnight. If you're short on time, you can roast the lamb and potatoes together on a sheet pan at 200°C for 12–15 minutes, though keep an eye on the potatoes so they don't burn. For the lamb, an instant-read thermometer is your friend: 52°C for rare, 58°C for medium. Always rest the meat before slicing to keep it juicy.
