# Moroccan-Spiced Roasted Cauliflower with Chickpeas and Lemon Tahini Yogurt

Cauliflower florets roasted hard in a rust-coloured spice paste until the edges char, then cooled by a lemony tahini yogurt swoosh.

## Ingredients

(Makes 2 servings)

- 600 g (about 1 medium head) cauliflower, cut into florets
- 240 g canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed (1 x 400 g tin)
- 1.5 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp tahini
- 200 g 0% fat Greek yogurt
- 15 g flaked almonds
- 40 g pomegranate seeds
- 1 lemon — juice for the sauce, zest for finishing
- 1 garlic clove, finely grated
- 2 tsp ground cumin
- 1.5 tsp ground coriander
- 1 tsp smoked paprika
- 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
- 1/4 tsp cayenne pepper
- 1 tsp fine salt, plus a pinch for the sauce
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 10 g cilantro or flat-leaf parsley, torn

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 220°C (425°F) with a rack in the upper third. Line a large rimmed baking sheet with parchment. The pan needs to hold the cauliflower in one layer with visible gaps — crowded florets release steam and turn soft instead of caramelised.
2. Break the cauliflower into 4 cm florets, keeping a little of the pale stem attached so each piece stays intact. Spread them on a clean tea towel and pat bone dry. Surface water is the single biggest reason roasted cauliflower comes out pale and limp.
3. In a large bowl, whisk the olive oil with the cumin, coriander, smoked paprika, cinnamon, turmeric, cayenne, 3/4 tsp salt and the black pepper into a loose, rust-coloured paste. Add the cauliflower and toss with your hands, working the paste into every crevice until the florets look dusted with brick-coloured sand.
4. Tip the cauliflower onto the baking sheet and set the pieces cut-side down, spaced apart. Roast for 15 minutes without touching them — the flat faces should weld to the parchment and caramelise.
5. While that roasts, drain and rinse the chickpeas, then dry them on the same towel. Toss them in the empty spice bowl to pick up the residue. After 15 minutes, pull the pan, scatter the chickpeas around the cauliflower, shake gently and roast for a further 12–14 minutes, until the cauliflower edges are deep mahogany and a knife slides through a stem with no resistance.
6. Make the sauce: whisk the tahini with the lemon juice in a small bowl. It will seize into a grainy, stiff paste — this is normal. Keep whisking for 30 seconds and it loosens. Stir in the Greek yogurt, grated garlic, a pinch of salt and the lemon zest, then add 1–2 tbsp cold water until the sauce falls from the whisk in a thick ribbon.
7. Toast the flaked almonds in a dry skillet over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, tossing often, until they smell warm and nutty and the edges turn pale gold. Slide them onto a cold plate straight away; they go from gold to bitter in under a minute.
8. Spread the tahini yogurt across a platter in a wide swoosh. Pile the hot cauliflower and chickpeas on top so the sauce warms at the edges. Scatter over the pomegranate seeds, toasted almonds and torn herbs, then finish with a squeeze of lemon and a thread of olive oil.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 552 kcal
- Protein: 29 g
- Carbohydrates: 55 g
- Fat: 27 g
- Fiber: 16 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

If you have a jar of ras el hanout, use 2 tbsp of it in place of the individual spices — the blend already carries the same cumin-cinnamon-rose profile. For more protein, add 200 g of cubed halloumi in the last 10 minutes of roasting, or swap the chickpeas for 300 g of cooked white beans. The tahini sauce is deliberately thick; thin it with water, never more lemon, or it will split. Leftovers keep 3 days in the fridge — store the sauce separately and reheat the cauliflower and chickpeas at 200°C for 6–8 minutes to bring the edges back to crisp. Cold, straight from the tub, it also makes a good lunch with a handful of rocket.
