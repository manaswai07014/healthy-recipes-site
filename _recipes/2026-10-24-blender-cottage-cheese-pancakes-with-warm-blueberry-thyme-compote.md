# Blender Cottage Cheese Pancakes with Warm Blueberry-Thyme Compote

Blending cottage cheese until it's silk-smooth is the trick here — it keeps these pancakes custardy inside while oat flour gives them a crisp lace edge.

## Ingredients

(Makes 2 servings)

- 300 g low-fat (2%) cottage cheese
- 3 large eggs
- 60 g rolled oats
- 1 tsp baking powder
- 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp fine sea salt
- 1 tsp vanilla extract
- 1 tsp finely grated lemon zest, plus more to serve
- 200 g fresh blueberries
- 100 g fresh raspberries
- 1 tsp honey
- 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 2 tsp extra-virgin olive oil, for the pan
- 100 g 0% fat Greek yogurt

## Method

1. Step 1 — Build a silky base: Tip the cottage cheese, eggs, vanilla, lemon zest and salt into a blender. Run on high for 45-60 seconds, stopping once to scrape the sides, until the mixture is completely free of visible curds. It should pour like heavy cream and feel slick, not grainy, when you rub a drop between your fingers. Any remaining curd will show up as a lump in the finished pancake.
2. Step 2 — Make oat flour: Wipe the blender jar dry or use a spice grinder. Pulse the rolled oats for 20-30 seconds until they collapse into a fine, pale flour — you should get about 50 g. Whisk it in a wide bowl with the baking powder and cinnamon so the leavener is evenly dispersed, not clumped in one corner.
3. Step 3 — Combine and rest: Pour the wet mixture into the dry and whisk for about 20 seconds, just until the last dry streaks disappear. The batter will be thick, like a loose muffin batter, and should ribbon slowly off the whisk. Let it stand 10 minutes at room temperature; the oat flour hydrates and the batter tightens. If it stiffens past scooping, loosen with 1-2 tbsp milk.
4. Step 4 — Simmer the compote: While the batter rests, combine the blueberries, raspberries, honey, lemon juice and thyme sprigs in a small saucepan over medium heat. After 2-3 minutes the berries begin to hiss and split; drop the heat to low and simmer 6-8 minutes, pressing roughly a third of the fruit against the pan with the back of a spoon. You are after a glossy syrup that coats the spoon, not a thin juice. Pull out the thyme sprigs and keep warm.
5. Step 5 — Test one pancake: Heat a nonstick skillet over medium-low for a full 2 minutes. Add 1 tsp olive oil and wipe it out with a paper towel so only a faint film remains — too much fat and the edges fry instead of setting. Ladle 1/4 cup (60 ml) batter per pancake. It should sizzle quietly, not spit. Cook 2.5-3 minutes, until the rims look dry and matte and small bubbles break across the surface, then flip and cook 1.5-2 minutes more. The pancake should feel springy and sound hollow when tapped.
6. Step 6 — Hold and repeat: Slide the finished pancakes onto a plate and keep them in a 90°C (200°F) oven while you cook the rest, adding the remaining 1 tsp oil and wiping the pan between batches. You will get 8 pancakes — 4 per person.
7. Step 7 — Plate: Stack 4 pancakes per plate, spoon the warm compote over so it runs into the layers, and add a 50 g dollop of Greek yogurt to each. Finish with a little extra lemon zest and a single thyme leaf. Eat immediately, while the outside is still crisp and the compote is hot enough to soften the top pancake.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 522 kcal
- Protein: 36 g
- Carbohydrates: 50 g
- Fat: 18 g
- Fiber: 9 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

Cottage cheese moisture swings wildly between brands, and that is the one variable that breaks this batter. If yours is loose or watery, drain it 10 minutes in a fine sieve, or add 1 tbsp oat flour. For a taller, airier stack, separate the eggs: blend the yolks with the cottage cheese, whip the whites to soft peaks, and fold them in after the dry ingredients — the pancakes rise noticeably but cook 30 seconds longer per side. Ricotta or 2% Greek yogurt both work in place of cottage cheese, though ricotta pushes the fat up by about 3 g per serving. Frozen berries need no thawing; just simmer them 2 minutes longer. Maple syrup can replace the honey one-for-one.

Storage: cooked pancakes keep 3 days covered in the fridge and reheat best in a dry skillet or a toaster — the microwave turns them rubbery. Freeze them flat between sheets of parchment for up to 2 months and reheat straight from frozen. The compote keeps a week in a jar; it is also good spooned over yogurt or oatmeal. Reheat it gently, since high heat dulls the thyme.
