# Provençal Saffron Fish Stew with Fennel, Cod & Shrimp

Saffron blooms in fennel-scented broth as cod and shrimp poach gently to 63°C. Tomato and orange zest deepen the Provençal aroma; olive oil finishes it.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 large fennel bulb, cored and thinly sliced
- 1 medium leek, white and light green parts only, thinly sliced
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/4 tsp saffron threads
- 1 strip orange zest
- 1 tsp sweet paprika
- 1/2 tsp fennel seeds
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 400 g canned chopped tomatoes
- 120 ml dry white wine
- 500 ml fish stock
- 400 g baby potatoes, halved
- 240 g canned cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
- 600 g cod fillets, cut into 4 cm chunks
- 200 g raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 1 tsp sea salt
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
- 2 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped, for garnish

## Method

1. Step 1: Prep the aromatics. Core the fennel, reserving a few fronds for garnish. Slice the bulb paper-thin on a mandoline or with a sharp knife; you should have about 2 cups. Slice the leek, mince the garlic, and halve the potatoes. Warm 2 tbsp of the white wine in a small bowl, crumble in the saffron, and let it steep while you cook—the liquid will turn deep amber and smell floral.
2. Step 2: Set a Dutch oven or heavy pot over medium heat. Add the olive oil, fennel, leek, garlic, fennel seeds, paprika, 1/2 tsp salt, and a few grinds of pepper. Cook, stirring often, for 8–10 minutes, until the fennel is soft and translucent and the leek is sweet, not browned. If it starts to stick, add a splash of water.
3. Step 3: Push the vegetables to one side, add the tomato paste to the bare pot, and fry for 1 minute until it darkens to brick red. Pour in the saffron-wine, the remaining wine, and scrape up any browned bits. Add the chopped tomatoes, fish stock, orange zest, bay leaf, thyme, and potatoes. Bring to a gentle simmer, cover, and cook for 12–15 minutes, until a knife slides easily into the potatoes.
4. Step 4: Stir in the cannellini beans and simmer uncovered for 2 minutes to thicken the broth slightly. Taste and adjust salt—the broth should be savory, saffron-forward, and slightly sweet from the fennel. Remove the bay leaf and thyme sprigs.
5. Step 5: Nestle the cod chunks into the broth, leaving space between them. Reduce heat to low—the liquid should barely bubble. Cover and poach for 4–5 minutes. Add the shrimp, cover, and cook 2–3 minutes more, until the cod flakes at the edges and registers 63°C (145°F) on an instant-read thermometer, and the shrimp are opaque and curled. Do not boil, or the fish will toughen.
6. Step 6: Take the pot off the heat and rest for 2 minutes so the fish absorbs the saffron broth. Ladle into warm shallow bowls, spooning plenty of fennel, beans, and potatoes around the fish. Scatter with parsley and reserved fennel fronds. Serve with lemon wedges if you like.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 469 kcal
- Protein: 48 g
- Carbohydrates: 44 g
- Fat: 8 g
- Fiber: 9 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

For a deeper broth, mash a few of the cannellini beans against the side of the pot before adding the fish. If you can't find cod, use halibut, pollock, or haddock—all hold their shape. Mussels are a traditional addition; add 300g scrubbed mussels with the shrimp and cook until they open, discarding any that stay shut. Leftovers keep in the fridge for 2 days; reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of stock, never boil. The stew does not freeze well because the potatoes become grainy.
