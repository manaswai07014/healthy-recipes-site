# Red Lentil and Mint Soup with Sumac-Roasted Chickpeas

Silky red lentils collapse into a peppery-herb broth, while roasted chickpeas add crunch. A final mint infusion keeps the soup bright and fragrant.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 250 g red lentils
- 600 ml vegetable stock
- 400 ml water
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp dried mint
- 1/2 tsp ground sumac
- 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
- 240 g canned chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 15 g fresh mint leaves, finely chopped
- 30 g plain Greek yogurt
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges

## Method

1. Step 1: Heat the oven to 210°C fan. Pat the chickpeas thoroughly dry, then toss them with 1/2 tbsp olive oil, half the sumac, and a pinch of salt. Roast for 15-18 minutes until golden, crisp at the edges, and audible when shaken. Set aside.
2. Step 2: Meanwhile, warm the remaining 1/2 tbsp olive oil in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the onion and cook for 6-8 minutes, stirring often, until soft and translucent with pale golden edges. Add the garlic, cumin, dried mint, red pepper flakes, and remaining sumac; stir for 45 seconds until fragrant.
3. Step 3: Add the lentils, vegetable stock, water, and salt. Bring to a boil, then reduce the heat to a gentle simmer. Cook for 18-22 minutes, stirring every few minutes and scraping the bottom, until the lentils have broken down and the soup flows thickly from a spoon.
4. Step 4: Remove from the heat and let the soup stand for 3 minutes. Stir in the chopped fresh mint; it should wilt and darken slightly while the aroma rises without becoming cooked.
5. Step 5: Divide the soup among four bowls. Swirl 1 tbsp Greek yogurt into each serving, scatter over the sumac-roasted chickpeas, and serve with lemon wedges for acidity.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 428 kcal
- Protein: 21 g
- Carbohydrates: 56 g
- Fat: 12 g
- Fiber: 15 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

For a thicker, more polished texture, blend half the soup with an immersion blender, then return it to the pot and warm gently. This adds body without cream. If sumac is unavailable, substitute 1/2 tsp lemon zest plus a pinch of smoked paprika; the zest will need brief cooking to release its oils. Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3 days, loosening with a splash of stock when reheating. Add the crisp chickpeas only after serving so they retain their crunch.
