# Linguine with Shrimp, Mussels and Cherry Tomatoes in White Wine

Garlic sizzles in olive oil, wine hisses in the pan, mussels steam open — then the pasta finishes in that broth so every strand tastes of the sea.

## Ingredients

(Makes 2 servings)

- 130 g dry linguine (about two-thirds of a standard 200 g box)
- 1/2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 4 garlic cloves, sliced paper-thin
- 1/2 tsp red chili flakes
- 250 g cherry tomatoes, halved through the equator
- 120 ml dry white wine (Pinot Grigio, Vermentino or Sauvignon Blanc)
- 500 g mussels in their shells, scrubbed and debearded
- 200 g raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- Zest of 1 lemon, grated fine
- 15 g flat-leaf parsley, leaves chopped
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt, plus a handful for the pasta water
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper

## Method

1. Step 1 — Clean the shellfish. Rinse the 500 g mussels under cold running water, scrubbing each shell with a stiff brush to remove grit and barnacle flakes. Pull the beard toward the hinge and tug it out. Tap any shell that is already open against the counter: if it does not clamp shut within a few seconds, throw it away. Keep the mussels in a bowl of cold water in the fridge while you work. Pat the 200 g shrimp dry on paper towel and return them to the fridge — wet shrimp steam instead of sear.
2. Step 2 — Start the pasta water. Fill a large pot with 3 litres of water, add a generous handful of salt, and bring it to a hard rolling boil. Add the 130 g linguine and stir for the first 30 seconds so the strands do not fuse. Cook 1 minute short of the package time (roughly 8 minutes for a 9-minute pasta) — the pasta will finish in the sauce, and it needs to stay firm enough to absorb it.
3. Step 3 — Build the base. While the pasta boils, set a 30 cm skillet or sauté pan with a lid over medium-low heat. Add the 1/2 tbsp olive oil, the 4 sliced garlic cloves and the 1/2 tsp chili flakes. Let them sizzle for 45–60 seconds. You are listening for a steady, quiet crackle and smelling sweet garlic — the moment the edges turn pale gold, move on. Browned garlic turns acrid and will taste bitter through the whole dish.
4. Step 4 — Caramelise the tomatoes. Lay the 250 g halved cherry tomatoes in the pan cut-side down and raise the heat to medium. Leave them alone for 3 minutes so the cut faces blister and take on colour, then stir and cook 2 minutes more. The skins should wrinkle and pull away, and the pan should hold a shallow pool of pink-orange juice.
5. Step 5 — Deglaze with wine. Pour in the 120 ml dry white wine. It will hiss hard and throw up a sharp, alcoholic steam. Simmer for 2 minutes, scraping the base of the pan with a wooden spoon, until the smell turns floral and the liquid has reduced by about a third. The alcohol must cook off before the mussels go in, or the sauce tastes raw.
6. Step 6 — Steam the mussels. Tip in the drained mussels, clamp on the lid, and cook over medium-high for 3–4 minutes, shaking the pan once halfway. They are ready when the shells swing open easily and the meat inside is plump and orange-white. Lift them out with tongs into a bowl and set aside. Discard any that stayed shut — that is the reliable doneness test for shellfish.
7. Step 7 — Poach the shrimp. Add the 200 g shrimp to the wine broth in a single layer. Cook 60–90 seconds per side; they are done when they curl into a loose C shape and turn opaque white with pink edges. The thickest part should read 63°C on a thermometer. A tight, closed O means overcooked — pull them the second they firm up.
8. Step 8 — Finish the pasta in the sauce. Scoop out 1 cup of the starchy pasta water, then drain the linguine. Add the pasta to the pan with 1/2 cup of the pasta water and toss over medium heat for 1–2 minutes. The starch emulsifies with the wine and tomato juices until the sauce turns glossy and clings to each strand rather than pooling. If it looks tight, add pasta water a splash at a time.
9. Step 9 — Bring it together and serve. Return the mussels and any juices to the pan, add the lemon zest, chopped parsley and 1/4 tsp black pepper, and toss off the heat for 20 seconds — just long enough to warm the mussels through without toughening them. Divide between two warmed shallow bowls, spooning the pan liquid over the top, and eat immediately while the shells are still hot.

- Carbohydrates: 56 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

The technique that separates this from a watery seafood pasta is finishing the noodles in the pan, not the pot. That reserved pasta water is not optional — its starch is what turns wine and tomato juice into a sauce that coats. If you swap in littleneck clams, add 2 extra minutes of covered steaming; bay scallops or squid rings can replace the shrimp but need only 45–60 seconds per side. Frozen cooked mussel meat (about 150 g) works on a rushed weeknight: thaw, add it with the parsley at the end, and skip the steaming step entirely. For a richer version, swirl in 1 tsp of cold butter or mascarpone off the heat — it adds roughly 35 kcal per serving. To drop below 500 kcal, cut the pasta to 100 g total and add 100 g more cherry tomatoes. Leftovers keep 2 days in the fridge, but the mussels will firm up; reheat gently in a covered pan with 2 tbsp water, never in a microwave, which turns shrimp to rubber. Discard any mussel that refuses to open at any stage.
