# Burrata & Cherry Tomato Spaghetti

There's a moment when the burrata breaks open and everything changes—the sauce transforms from bright and chunky into something luxuriously velvety, each strand of pasta coated in that flowing cream. This is the kind of dish that makes people pause mid-bite. Cherry tomatoes, halved and roasted directly in their own juices, burst with concentrated sweetness against the herbal freshness of torn basil. The key is restraint: let the burrata do the heavy lifting for richness rather than drowning the dish in oil. With al dente spaghetti absorbing that chunky tomato sauce, this delivers the drama of restaurant pasta in under 40 minutes. Serve it family-style in the pan or plate it individually with the burrata as a centerpiece—either way, make sure everyone sees that satisfying moment when the cheese breaks.

## Ingredients

- 1.5 tbsp extra virgin olive oil
- 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 1 can (400g) whole San Marzano tomatoes
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1/4 cup fresh basil, torn, plus more for garnish
- 200g dried spaghetti
- 250g mixed cherry tomatoes, halved
- 3 tbsp grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
- 115g fresh burrata (1 ball)
- Freshly cracked black pepper

## Instructions

1. Place a wide saucepan over medium heat. Add the olive oil and swirl to coat the pan. Scatter in the sliced garlic and cook, stirring occasionally, for 60 to 90 seconds until the edges turn golden and the kitchen fills with a sweet, toasted aroma. Do not walk away—this moves fast.
2. Pour in the whole tomatoes, crushing them with a wooden spoon as they hit the pan. The liquid will sizzle dramatically; stand back briefly. Season with salt and add the torn basil. Bring to a vigorous simmer—you want active bubbling across the entire surface.
3. Reduce heat to medium-low, cover the pan with a lid tilted slightly open, and maintain that aggressive simmer for 10 to 12 minutes. The sauce should reduce by roughly one-third, coating the spoon thickly. For a saucier result, use an immersion blender for just 5 seconds—leave it chunky for better texture contrast.
4. While the sauce works, bring a large pot of heavily salted water to a rolling boil. Cook the spaghetti until 2 minutes before the package directs for al dente. Reserve 1/2 cup pasta water before draining.
5. Add the drained pasta directly to the tomato sauce in the saucepan. Toss vigorously, adding splashes of reserved pasta water to create a light emulsification that clings to every strand.
6. Divide between warm shallow bowls. Shower with Parmigiano-Reggiano and arrange the cherry tomato halves cut-side up around the perimeter. Place the burrata in the center, drizzle with olive oil, and finish with torn basil and cracked pepper. Instruct diners to pierce the burrata at the table so the creamy center mingles with the sauce.

## Chef's Tips

The pasta water is your secret weapon—its starchiness helps the sauce cling and creates a more cohesive dish. Add it gradually, tossing constantly, until you achieve a glossy coating rather than a watery pool. For the best burrata experience, bring it to room temperature 20 minutes before serving; cold burrata firms up the interior cream and loses that signature ooze. If cherry tomatoes aren't at peak ripeness, roast them briefly in a 400°F oven for 15 minutes before adding to the sauce—this concentrates their sweetness. Leftover components keep separately: the sauce refrigerated for 3 days, cooked pasta for 2 days, but the burrata should be consumed the same day for optimal texture. Reheat the sauce and pasta together with a splash of water, then crown with fresh burrata when serving.
