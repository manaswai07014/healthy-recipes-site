# Provençal Saffron and Fennel Fish Stew with Garlic-Rubbed Sourdough

Saffron blooms gold in warm stock while sliced fennel sweats down to sweetness. Cod then poaches gently in tomato until it flakes at 63°C.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 700 g firm white fish fillets (cod, hake or monkfish), cut into 5cm chunks
- 1 large fennel bulb, cored and sliced 5mm thick, fronds reserved
- 1 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
- 3 garlic cloves, 2 minced + 1 halved for the bread
- 1 1/2 tbsp olive oil
- 120 ml dry white wine
- 400 g canned chopped tomatoes
- 250 ml fish stock
- 300 g new potatoes, halved
- 1/2 tsp saffron threads
- 1 tsp fennel seeds
- 1/2 tsp sweet paprika
- 1 strip orange zest
- 60 g 0% Greek yogurt
- 1 tsp tomato paste
- 4 slices sourdough bread
- Salt and black pepper

## Method

1. Step 1: Crumble the saffron threads between your fingers into 60ml of the fish stock in a small bowl. Leave to steep for 10 minutes — the liquid should turn a deep, almost amber gold. This step is what separates a flat stew from one that tastes of the south of France.
2. Step 2: Set a wide, heavy pot (a 4-litre Dutch oven is ideal) over medium heat and add 1 tbsp of the olive oil. Add the sliced fennel and onion with a good pinch of salt. Sweat for 8-10 minutes, stirring every couple of minutes, until the slices turn translucent and collapse against the spoon. You are listening for a quiet sizzle, not a roar — if anything starts to brown, lower the heat. Browning here makes the broth bitter.
3. Step 3: Add the minced garlic, fennel seeds, paprika and orange zest. Stir constantly for 45 seconds. The fennel seeds will start to pop against the pot and the paprika will darken the oil to a rust colour. Do not walk away — garlic goes from sweet to acrid in under a minute.
4. Step 4: Pour in the white wine and scrape the base of the pot with a wooden spoon to lift anything stuck. Simmer for 3 minutes until the sharp alcohol smell disappears and the wine has reduced to a thin syrup.
5. Step 5: Add the chopped tomatoes, the remaining stock, the saffron with its steeping liquid, and the potatoes. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook uncovered for 12-14 minutes. The sauce is ready when it has darkened, thickened slightly, and a knife slides into a potato half with no resistance at all.
6. Step 6: Pat the fish dry and season it lightly with salt. Nestle the chunks into the sauce in a single layer, spooning a little sauce over the top. Cover the pot, drop the heat to its lowest setting, and poach for 6-8 minutes. You want lazy bubbles at the edge of the pot, never a rolling boil — a hard boil will shred the fish. The fish is done when it is opaque throughout and separates into flakes at 63°C on an instant-read thermometer.
7. Step 7: While the fish poaches, toast the sourdough until the edges are crisp and the centre still gives slightly. Rub each slice once with the cut garlic clove and set aside.
8. Step 8: Take the pot off the heat. Whisk the yogurt with the tomato paste and a spoonful of the hot broth in a small bowl to temper it, then swirl it through the stew. Let it sit for 2 minutes — the broth will turn slightly creamy and pink. Scatter over the reserved fennel fronds, grind over black pepper, drizzle with the remaining 1/2 tbsp olive oil, and serve with the garlic toast.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 440 kcal
- Protein: 41 g
- Carbohydrates: 46 g
- Fat: 9 g
- Fiber: 6 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

Monkfish is the most forgiving choice here because its dense muscle holds shape through the poach; cod and hake are more delicate and will flake apart the moment they are overcooked, so pull them at 63°C and no later. If you want to push the protein higher, add 200g of scrubbed mussels or clams in Step 6, cover the pot and cook until the shells open, discarding any that stay shut. Saffron is not interchangeable with turmeric, but if you are out of it, 1/4 tsp turmeric plus an extra pinch of paprika will give colour without the floral note.

The stew keeps for 2 days in the fridge, but reheat it gently over low heat — a boil will break the yogurt emulsion and toughen the fish. If you are making it ahead, cook through Step 5, chill, then poach the fish fresh just before serving. The broth alone freezes well for up to 2 months.
