# High-Protein Turkish Red Lentil Soup with Mint & Chicken

Velvety red lentil soup with dried mint and cumin, enriched with shredded chicken. Simmer, blend, then fold in chicken for a high-protein bowl.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 1 cup dried red lentils
- 1 lb boneless, skinless chicken breast
- 1 large onion, diced
- 1 medium carrot, diced
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp dried mint
- 1/2 tsp paprika
- 6 cups low-sodium chicken broth
- 1/2 cup Greek yogurt
- 1 lemon, cut into wedges
- 1/4 cup fresh mint leaves, chopped

## Method

1. Step 1: Rinse the red lentils under cold water until the water runs clear, picking out any stones. Drain well and set aside.
2. Step 2: Warm the olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add the diced onion and carrot. Cook, stirring occasionally, for 5–7 minutes until the onion is translucent and the carrot softens. You should hear a gentle sizzle, not a fierce crackle.
3. Step 3: Push the vegetables to one side of the pot. Add the tomato paste, cumin, paprika, and dried mint to the cleared space. Fry for 60 seconds until the paste darkens and smells sweet and smoky. Stir it into the vegetables.
4. Step 4: Add the rinsed lentils and chicken broth. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a simmer. Add the diced chicken breast. Cover and simmer for 20–25 minutes, until the lentils are completely soft and the chicken reaches 75°C (165°F) on an instant-read thermometer.
5. Step 5: Using tongs, remove the chicken to a plate. Blend the soup with an immersion blender until smooth and velvety. If it’s too thick, add a splash of broth.
6. Step 6: Shred the chicken with two forks, then return it to the pot. Stir in the lemon juice, salt, and pepper. Taste—it should be bright, earthy, and minty.
7. Step 7: Ladle into bowls. Top each with a dollop of Greek yogurt and a scatter of fresh mint. Serve with lemon wedges.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 485 kcal
- Protein: 52 g
- Carbohydrates: 39 g
- Fat: 12 g
- Fiber: 7 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

For a vegetarian version, omit the chicken and add 1 can (400g) drained chickpeas; use vegetable broth. That adds about 7g protein per serving. The soup thickens as it sits; thin with broth when reheating. Store in the fridge up to 4 days, or freeze up to 3 months. Dried mint is essential—fresh mint won’t give the same penetrating aroma. If you don’t have dried mint, crush fresh mint with a pinch of salt and add at the end.
