# Burrata with Chunky Tomato Sauce and Fresh Basil

There's something almost theatrical about tearing into fresh burrata at the table—the cream that spills out, the way it transforms a simple tomato sauce into something luxurious. This recipe captures that magic with minimal effort. San Marzano tomatoes, crushed directly in the pan, release their bright acidity into a jammy, deeply flavored sauce that tastes like it spent hours on the stove. The key is patience: letting the sauce bubble uncovered so the liquid evaporates and the flavors concentrate. Meanwhile, spaghetti cooks to that perfect al dente state—firm enough to hold its shape when tossed with the chunky sauce. A shower of Parmesan, a cascade of halved cherry tomatoes, and a whole burrata placed at the center complete the picture. When you break through the delicate exterior, the interior blends seamlessly into the pasta, creating ribbons of richness in every bite.

## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, divided
- 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 1 can (28 oz) whole San Marzano tomatoes
- 1 tsp kosher salt
- 1/2 cup loosely packed fresh basil leaves, roughly torn
- 4 oz dried spaghetti
- 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
- 2 tbsp freshly grated Parmesan cheese
- 6 oz fresh burrata cheese (1.5 balls)
- 1/4 tsp freshly cracked black pepper

## Instructions

1. In a wide saucepan, warm 1.5 tablespoons of olive oil over medium heat until it shimmers. Add the sliced garlic and let it sizzle for 45 to 60 seconds, watching carefully until the edges turn golden and the aroma turns sweet and mellow. Pour in the whole tomatoes with their liquid—the mixture will bubble aggressively, so stand back slightly. Use a potato masher or the back of a wooden spoon to crush the tomatoes into irregular chunks, leaving some pieces purposefully rough. Stir in the salt and scatter half the basil across the top.
2. Reduce the heat to medium-low and bring the sauce to a vigorous simmer. Partially cover with a lid tilted to allow steam to escape, and cook for 10 to 12 minutes until the sauce deepens in color and coats the back of a spoon. During the final minute, stir in the remaining basil. Taste and adjust seasoning—it should taste bold and slightly sweet.
3. While the sauce reduces, bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil. Season generously with salt—it should taste like the sea. Cook the spaghetti until it holds a slight bite at the center, about 2 minutes shy of package directions. Reserve 1/2 cup of the starchy pasta water before draining.
4. Add the drained pasta directly to the tomato sauce. Toss vigorously, adding splashes of the reserved pasta water to create a glossy coating that clings to every strand. The sauce should cling to the noodles, not pool at the bottom.
5. Plate immediately: nestle the pasta in a wide shallow bowl. Shower with Parmesan curls and arrange the cherry tomato halves around the edge. Place the burrata in the center and drizzle with the remaining half tablespoon of olive oil. Finish with cracked pepper and a generous pinch of flaky salt. At the table, pierce the burrata and watch the cream spill across the top before tossing gently to distribute.
6. Alternative method: For a family-style presentation, combine everything in the pot over low heat, breaking the burrata into large pieces and folding it through just before serving. The residual warmth will soften the cheese without melting it entirely.

## Chef's Tips

For a protein boost and extra fiber, substitute half the spaghetti with chickpea pasta or whole wheat spaghetti—the sauce clings beautifully to both. If cherry tomatoes are out of season, skip them and double the fresh basil instead; the dish remains vibrant without losing balance. Leftovers keep refrigerated for up to 2 days, but note that the burrata should be omitted initially and added fresh when reheating individual portions. Store the saucy pasta separately in an airtight container, then warm gently over medium-low heat with a splash of water to loosen before plating and topping with cold burrata.
