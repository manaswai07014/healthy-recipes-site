# Italian Turkey-Stuffed Bell Peppers with Brown Rice and Mozzarella

Halved peppers roast cut-side down first, so they blister at the edges instead of steaming in their own juice — then fennel-scented turkey and brown rice go in.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 4 large red and yellow bell peppers
- 450 g (1 lb) lean ground turkey, 93% lean
- 1 cup cooked brown rice
- 1 small zucchini, coarsely grated
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 medium yellow onion, finely diced
- 3 garlic cloves, minced
- 1 tsp fennel seed, lightly crushed
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes
- 1 1/2 cups no-sugar-added marinara sauce
- 30 g Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
- 60 g part-skim mozzarella, shredded
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 10 g fresh basil leaves, torn

## Method

1. Step 1: Heat the oven to 200°C (400°F) with a rack in the middle. Halve the peppers lengthwise straight through the stem. Scoop out the seeds and the pale white ribs with a spoon — leaving the ribs in makes the finished dish bitter and watery. Rub the cut faces with 1 tsp of the olive oil and season with a pinch of the salt. Set them cut-side down on a parchment-lined sheet pan and roast for 12 minutes, until the skins blister in dark spots and a spoon pressed into the flesh meets gentle resistance. Pull them out and leave the oven on.
2. Step 2: While the peppers roast, heat the remaining 2 tsp oil in a large skillet over medium-high. Add the onion and cook for 4 minutes, stirring occasionally, until translucent with gold edges. Add the garlic, crushed fennel seed, oregano and red pepper flakes and stir constantly for 30 seconds — the fennel should smell like sweet anise the moment it blooms.
3. Step 3: Add the turkey and break it into walnut-sized crumbles with a wooden spoon. Leave it alone for 2 minutes at a time between stirs so the pieces brown rather than steam grey. Cook 6–7 minutes total, until no pink remains and the meat reads 75°C (165°F) on an instant-read thermometer. Season with the black pepper and half the remaining salt.
4. Step 4: Wrap the grated zucchini in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze hard until it stops dripping — this is what stops the filling from turning the peppers into soup. Add the drained zucchini, cooked brown rice and 1 cup of the marinara to the skillet. Simmer 3 minutes, until a spoon dragged through the mixture leaves a clean line behind it. Off the heat, stir in the Parmigiano-Reggiano. Taste and adjust salt; the filling should read slightly saltier than you want, because the peppers dilute it.
5. Step 5: Blot the roasted pepper halves dry inside with paper towel. Spoon the filling in, mounding it slightly and packing gently so no air pockets remain. Arrange them in a baking dish just large enough to hold them snugly. Pour the remaining 1/2 cup marinara around the base of the peppers, not over the tops, and cover the dish tightly with foil.
6. Step 6: Bake for 15 minutes. Uncover, scatter the mozzarella over the tops, and bake another 5–7 minutes, until the cheese has collapsed into a glossy sheet and bubbles at the edges. If you want browning, switch on the broiler for 90 seconds and watch it — mozzarella goes from spotted to burnt in under a minute.
7. Step 7: Rest the dish for 5 minutes so the filling sets and the pan sauce thickens. Scatter the torn basil over the top and spoon the tomato from the base of the dish over each pepper before serving.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 443 kcal
- Protein: 32 g
- Carbohydrates: 30 g
- Fat: 19 g
- Fiber: 6 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

The fennel seed is doing the heavy lifting here: crushed and bloomed in oil, it reads as Italian sausage without any of the pork fat, which is how this lands at 19g fat per serving instead of 35g. If you keep fennel pollen or whole fennel in the cupboard, use either — crush whole seeds under the flat of a knife first, or they stay hard and taste like licorice splinters. Swapping the brown rice for cooked farro adds chew and about 20 kcal per serving; cooked cauliflower rice drops it by roughly 40 kcal but you should then reduce the marinara to 1 cup, since cauliflower releases more water. For a deeper flavour, brown the turkey a shade harder than feels comfortable — the fond on the pan bottom is what makes the tomato taste cooked-for-hours. Leftovers keep 4 days in a sealed container; reheat covered at 175°C (350°F) for 15 minutes, or microwave loosely covered, which softens the pepper further but keeps the cheese from toughening. These freeze well for 3 months — freeze before adding the mozzarella, thaw overnight, then top and bake.
