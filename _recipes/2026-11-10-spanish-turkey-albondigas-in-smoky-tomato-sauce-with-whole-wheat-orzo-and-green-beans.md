# Spanish Turkey Albondigas in Smoky Tomato Sauce with Whole Wheat Orzo and Green Beans

Sizzling turkey albondigas browned in olive oil then simmered in smoked paprika tomato sauce. The sauce clings to each meatball; orzo soaks up the rest.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 500g lean ground turkey (93% lean)
- 1 large egg white
- 30g whole wheat breadcrumbs
- 2 tbsp finely grated onion
- 2 garlic cloves, minced
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh parsley
- 2 tsp smoked paprika, divided
- 1/2 tsp ground cumin
- 1/2 tsp salt
- 1/4 tsp black pepper
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 medium onion, chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, sliced
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 400g canned crushed tomatoes
- 1/2 tsp dried oregano
- 1 bay leaf
- 240ml low-sodium chicken broth
- 1 tbsp sherry vinegar
- 1/2 tsp sugar
- 2 cups dry whole wheat orzo
- 400g green beans, trimmed
- 1 tbsp salt for pasta water

## Method

1. Step 1: In a large bowl, combine the ground turkey, egg white, breadcrumbs, grated onion, minced garlic, parsley, 1 tsp of the smoked paprika, cumin, salt, and black pepper. Mix with your hands just until evenly combined — overmixing makes the meatballs dense. Roll into 16 meatballs, about 4 per serving. Chill for 10 minutes if you have time; this helps them hold shape.
2. Step 2: Heat the olive oil in a large skillet over medium heat. Brown the meatballs in a single layer, working in batches if needed, for 2–3 minutes per side until golden on the outside but not cooked through. They will finish in the sauce. Transfer to a plate. You should hear a steady sizzle; if the pan smokes, lower the heat.
3. Step 3: In the same skillet, add the chopped onion and cook for 5 minutes, stirring, until soft and translucent. Add the sliced garlic, tomato paste, remaining 1 tsp smoked paprika, and oregano. Cook for 1 minute until fragrant — the tomato paste will darken slightly and smell sweet.
4. Step 4: Pour in the crushed tomatoes, chicken broth, sherry vinegar, and sugar. Add the bay leaf. Bring to a gentle simmer, scraping up any browned bits. Return the meatballs to the skillet, cover, and simmer gently for 15–18 minutes, turning once, until the meatballs register 75°C (165°F) on an instant-read thermometer and the sauce has thickened enough to coat the back of a spoon. Discard the bay leaf. Taste and adjust salt.
5. Step 5: While the meatballs simmer, bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add the whole wheat orzo and cook according to package directions, usually 8–10 minutes, until al dente. Drain well. At the same time, steam the green beans in a steamer basket over boiling water for 4–5 minutes, until bright green and crisp-tender.
6. Step 6: Divide the orzo among 4 plates or shallow bowls. Top with the meatballs and a generous ladle of tomato sauce. Serve the green beans alongside. Garnish with extra parsley if you like.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 527 kcal
- Protein: 39 g
- Carbohydrates: 62 g
- Fat: 14 g
- Fiber: 10 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

For a Spanish touch, add a pinch of saffron threads to the chicken broth before it goes into the sauce. If you prefer a lower-carb plate, swap the orzo for cauliflower rice or steamed zucchini ribbons — the meatballs and sauce stay the same. The meatballs can be made with lean ground chicken (cook to 75°C) or lean ground pork (cook to 70°C); both work, though pork will be slightly richer. Store leftovers in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 3 days. The sauce and meatballs freeze well for up to 2 months; thaw overnight and reheat gently on the stovetop with a splash of broth. If the tomato sauce tastes sharp, add another pinch of sugar; if it tastes flat, a few extra drops of sherry vinegar will wake it up.
