# Lemon-Herb Seared Scallops over Garlicky Zucchini Noodles

Hard sear, soft herbs: scallops hit a screaming-hot pan for 90 seconds a side, then land on snappy zucchini ribbons glossed with lemon-tarragon butter.

## Ingredients

(Makes 2 servings)

- 340 g large dry-packed sea scallops (about 10–12)
- 2 medium zucchini (600 g), spiralized into 3 mm noodles
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 tsp unsalted butter
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 large lemon (zest plus 2 tbsp juice)
- 100 g cherry tomatoes, halved
- 2 tbsp dry white wine
- 15 g Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
- 20 g pine nuts
- 10 g flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- 5 g fresh tarragon, chopped
- 10 g fresh basil, torn
- 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt, plus more for the zucchini
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper

## Method

1. Step 1 — Dry the zucchini. Spiralize the zucchini into 3 mm noodles and toss with 1/4 tsp salt in a colander set over the sink. Leave 10 minutes, then gather in a clean kitchen towel and squeeze firmly — you should wring out 2–3 tbsp of water. Skipping this is why most zucchini-noodle plates end up as soup.
2. Step 2 — Dry the scallops. Pat each scallop bone dry with paper towel and pull off the small tough side muscle. Stand them on a plate lined with fresh paper towel, uncovered, while you prep everything else. A wet scallop steams; a dry one browns.
3. Step 3 — Toast the pine nuts. Put a 12-inch heavy skillet (cast iron or stainless, not nonstick) over medium heat. Add the pine nuts and shake for 2–3 minutes, until they smell faintly of popcorn and turn pale gold. Tip out onto a plate to cool.
4. Step 4 — Build the herb mix. In a small bowl, rub the lemon zest into the chopped parsley, tarragon, basil and red pepper flakes with your fingertips until it smells sharp and green. Keep the lemon juice in a separate cup.
5. Step 5 — Sear the scallops. Heat 2 tsp of the olive oil in the skillet over high heat until it shimmers and sends up the first thin wisp of smoke. Season the scallops with salt and pepper on one side only and lay them in, seasoned side down, 2 cm apart. You want a hard, continuous sizzle — a sputter means the pan is too cool. Do not move them for 90 seconds, until a deep amber crust forms. Flip, drop in the butter and sliced garlic, and spoon the foaming butter over the tops for 60–90 seconds, until the scallops are opaque through and register at least 63°C/145°F at the centre. Move to a warm plate.
6. Step 6 — Make the pan sauce. Take the skillet off the heat, pour in the white wine and scrape the browned fond loose with a wooden spoon. Add the lemon juice and any juices that have pooled under the scallops. Return to medium heat and reduce for about 30 seconds, until slightly syrupy. Pour into a small bowl and wipe the pan.
7. Step 7 — Flash-cook the noodles. Add the remaining 1 tsp olive oil to the skillet over high heat, add the drained zucchini noodles and toss constantly for 60–90 seconds — just until they lose their raw snap but still bite back. Add the cherry tomatoes for the final 30 seconds. Season with salt and pepper and divide between two warmed bowls.
8. Step 8 — Plate. Spoon the pan sauce over the noodles, set the scallops on top, then scatter over the lemon-herb mix, toasted pine nuts and grated Parmigiano-Reggiano. Eat immediately, while the crust is still crisp.

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

The single biggest variable here is the scallop itself. Ask for dry-packed (sometimes labelled 'chemical-free' or 'untreated') scallops. Wet-packed scallops are soaked in sodium tripolyphosphate, which plumps them with water that no amount of paper towel will remove — they will release a puddle and grey-steam instead of crusting. If only wet-packed are available, soak them in cold water for 10 minutes, dry them thoroughly, then salt them and let them sit uncovered in the fridge for 20 minutes before searing. The same technique works for large shrimp (2 minutes per side) or a thick cod loin (4 minutes per side, 63°C/145°F), though cod will not give you the same caramelised edge.

On swaps and storage: no spiralizer means a julienne peeler or the coarse side of a box grater, but squeeze the strands harder — grated zucchini releases more water. Tarragon is the classic French partner for scallops; dill or chervil will do, and basil alone is fine if you want it more Italian. Zucchini noodles do not keep well once dressed, so store the cooked noodles and the scallops separately, no more than 2 days, and reheat the noodles in a dry hot pan for 60 seconds to drive off the liquid they shed. Scallops themselves are best the moment they leave the pan — reheating turns them rubbery, so cook only what you plan to eat.
