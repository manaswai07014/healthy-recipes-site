# Burst Cherry Tomato Pasta with Burrata

There's something undeniably theatrical about tearing into burrata at the dinner table—that first split releases a river of creamy stracciatella that mingles with bright, burst cherry tomatoes and al dente pasta. This dish channels the spirit of a Roman trattoria: unpretentious ingredients elevated through technique and timing. The sauce comes together in the time it takes to boil water, its depth built from caramelized garlic and tomatoes cooked until they surrender their sweetness. Fresh basil adds herbaceous brightness, while the burrata provides that luxurious, almost buttery richness that transforms weeknight cooking into something worthy of a special occasion. Serve it family-style, letting everyone build their own perfect bite.

## Ingredients

- 2 tbsp olive oil for the pan
- 3 cloves garlic, sliced thin
- 1 can whole peeled tomatoes
- 1/2 tsp kosher salt
- 1/4 cup torn basil
- 6 oz spaghetti or bucatini
- 1 cup halved cherry tomatoes
- 2 tbsp grated Parmesan
- 4 oz burrata (1 ball)
- Black pepper for finishing

## Instructions

1. Heat olive oil in a wide saucepan over medium heat. Add sliced garlic and watch carefully as it sizzles—about 30 seconds until the edges turn golden and the kitchen fills with that sweet, mellow aroma. Pour in the whole tomatoes with their liquid (stand back as it may spatter). Use a potato masher to break tomatoes into irregular chunks, leaving plenty of rustic texture.
2. Season with salt and add most of the basil, reserving a pinch for garnish. Bring to an aggressive simmer, then partially cover with the lid slightly offset to allow steam to escape. Let bubble vigorously for 10-12 minutes until the sauce deepens in color and thickens noticeably—it should coat a spoon. For a smoother consistency, blend briefly with an immersion blender, though the chunky texture is worth preserving.
3. While the sauce reduces, cook pasta in generously salted boiling water. Start tasting 2 minutes before the package timer finishes—the pasta should offer slight resistance when bitten. Reserve 1/4 cup pasta water before draining.
4. Add drained pasta directly to the tomato sauce. Toss vigorously, adding splashes of reserved pasta water to create a silky emulsion that clings to every strand. Transfer to a warmed platter or shallow bowl.
5. Scatter halved cherry tomatoes around the edges. Grate Parmesan generously over the center. Place the burrata in the middle—let each person tear it themselves at the table for that dramatic, creamy moment, or break it into pieces and fold gently through the pasta. Finish with reserved basil, cracked pepper, and a final drizzle of olive oil.

## Chef's Tips

For the most flavorful sauce, use high-quality San Marzano tomatoes—their lower acidity and meatier flesh create a naturally sweet base. The cherry tomatoes aren't strictly necessary when using whole canned tomatoes, but they add pops of color and a fresh, jammy sweetness that balances the dish. If burrata is unavailable, fresh mozzarella torn into pieces works, though you'll miss that creamy center—add a tablespoon of ricotta to restore some of that richness. Leftovers keep beautifully for 2 days; store the burrata separately and add fresh when reheating. The pasta will absorb sauce overnight, so add a splash of water when reheating to restore moisture.
