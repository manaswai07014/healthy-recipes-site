# Provençal Cod Stew with Fennel, Saffron and Yogurt Rouille

Fennel sweats to silk in olive oil, saffron blooms in warm wine, cod poaches gently to 63°C — a Provençal bourride lightened with beans and yogurt.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 700 g skinless cod fillet, cut into 4 cm chunks
- 1 large fennel bulb (350 g), cored and thinly sliced
- 1 medium leek, white and pale green parts, thinly sliced
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil + 1 tsp for the toast
- ½ tsp saffron threads
- 1 tsp fennel seeds, lightly crushed
- ½ tsp smoked paprika
- 1 pinch cayenne pepper
- 400 g canned chopped tomatoes
- 150 ml dry white wine
- 300 ml fish stock
- 1 strip orange zest (5 cm)
- 1 bay leaf
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme
- 240 g cooked cannellini beans, drained and rinsed
- 120 g 2% Greek yogurt
- 1 small garlic clove, grated (for the rouille)
- 1 tbsp lemon juice
- 4 thin slices wholemeal baguette (60 g)
- 2 tbsp flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- Sea salt and black pepper

## Method

1. Bloom the saffron first: crumble the threads between your fingers into a small cup, add 2 tbsp of the white wine and let it steep while you prep. Within 10 minutes the liquid turns deep amber and smells of honey and hay — this is the flavour backbone of the pot.
2. Warm 1 tbsp olive oil in a wide, heavy saucepan or shallow casserole over medium heat. Add the fennel and leek with a good pinch of salt. Sweat for 10–12 minutes, stirring every couple of minutes, until the slices collapse and turn translucent with a few gold edges. You want softness, not colour — if they start browning, drop the heat.
3. Push the vegetables to the side, add the sliced garlic, crushed fennel seeds, smoked paprika and cayenne to the bare pan. Stir for 45 seconds until fragrant and the garlic smells sweet rather than sharp — do not let it brown or the stew turns bitter.
4. Pour in the remaining white wine plus the saffron soaking liquid. Simmer briskly for 2–3 minutes until the raw alcohol smell disappears and the liquid has reduced by about half.
5. Add the tomatoes, fish stock, orange zest, bay leaf and thyme. Bring to a gentle simmer, then cook uncovered for 12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce thickens enough to hold a trail behind a spoon. Taste and season with salt and pepper — it should be a touch saltier now than you want the finished stew, because the fish will dilute it.
6. Stir in the cannellini beans and let them heat through for 2 minutes. They thicken the broth and give it a creamy, almost velvety body without cream.
7. Lower the heat so the surface barely trembles. Nestle the cod chunks into the sauce in a single layer, spoon a little broth over the top, cover and poach for 6–8 minutes. The fish is done when it registers 63°C at the centre and separates into opaque, pearly flakes under light pressure — check at 6 minutes, since cod goes from silky to cottony fast.
8. While the fish poaches, whisk the Greek yogurt with the grated garlic, lemon juice, a pinch of salt and a few drops of olive oil into a loose, ivory rouille. Toast the baguette slices, brush with the remaining 1 tsp olive oil, and rub each one with the cut side of a garlic clove if you have a spare.
9. Take the pot off the heat and rest it, covered, for 3 minutes so the fish firms and the broth settles. Discard the bay leaf and thyme stems. Ladle into shallow bowls, spoon a generous dollop of rouille on top, scatter with parsley and serve with the toast for dragging through the saffron-stained broth.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 420 kcal
- Protein: 44 g
- Carbohydrates: 42 g
- Fat: 8 g
- Fiber: 9 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

Hake, pollack, monkfish or halibut all work here; keep the chunks thick so they poach rather than disintegrate. For a more traditional bourride, whisk a spoonful of the hot broth into the yogurt before it hits the bowl — it tempers the yogurt and stops it from splitting. If you prefer a starchier base, swap the cannellini beans for 300 g of waxy potatoes cut into 2 cm cubes and simmer them in the sauce for 15 minutes before the fish goes in. A splash of pastis instead of the wine, or a dozen scrubbed mussels added with the cod, pushes it further into Marseille territory.

The stew keeps 2 days in the fridge in a sealed container, but reheat it gently over low heat with a splash of stock — boiling will toughen the fish. The rouille is best made fresh, though it holds for 24 hours. To make it gluten-free, skip the baguette and serve with a spoon of steamed rice or a slice of toasted gluten-free sourdough.
