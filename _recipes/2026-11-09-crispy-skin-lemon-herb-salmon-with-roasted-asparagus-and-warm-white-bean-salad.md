# Crispy-Skin Lemon Herb Salmon with Roasted Asparagus and Warm White Bean Salad

Cold salmon hits a screaming-hot pan; the skin crackles, lemon zest blooms in the oil, and 40 minutes later dinner is on the table.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 4 skin-on salmon fillets (140 g / 5 oz each), pin-boned
- 600 g (1.3 lb) thick asparagus spears
- 2 x 400 g cans cannellini beans, drained and rinsed (about 400 g drained)
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- 1 large lemon — zest and about 3 tbsp juice
- 3 garlic cloves — 2 sliced, 1 grated
- 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
- 1 tbsp capers, rinsed
- 2 tbsp chopped fresh dill
- 2 tbsp chopped flat-leaf parsley
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes
- 1 tsp fine sea salt, plus more for the beans
- 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper

## Method

1. Dry-brine the salmon first. Pat each fillet bone-dry with paper towel, lay skin-side up on a plate, and sprinkle 3/4 tsp of the salt evenly over flesh and skin. Leave at room temperature for 15 minutes. Beads of moisture will rise to the surface — blot them off again right before cooking. Removing that surface water is the single thing that decides whether the skin shatters or steams.
2. Heat the oven to 220°C (425°F) with a rack in the upper third. Snap the woody ends off the asparagus — they break at their natural point, so bend each spear and let it tell you where to stop. Pile them on a large rimmed baking sheet, drizzle with 1 tbsp of the olive oil, add a pinch of salt and a few grinds of pepper, and roll them with your hands until every spear is glossy.
3. Spread the asparagus in a single layer with space between the spears. Crowding traps steam and gives you limp stalks. Roast for 10–12 minutes, until the tips are darkened and frizzled and the spears bend under their own weight but still bite back. You will hear them hissing when you open the door. Pull the tray out and leave it on the stovetop.
4. While the asparagus roasts, set a 30 cm (12 in) stainless-steel or cast-iron skillet over medium-high heat for a full 2 minutes. Test with a drop of water: it should evaporate on contact with a sizzle. Add 1 tsp of the olive oil and swirl it across the base.
5. Lay the fillets in the pan skin-side down, working away from you, and press each one flat with a spatula for 20 seconds so the skin makes full contact. Then leave them completely alone for 5–6 minutes. Watch the side of the fillet: the flesh turns opaque from the bottom upward, and the skin releases on its own when it is crisp. If it sticks, it is not ready — give it another 30 seconds.
6. Lower the heat to medium and flip the fillets. Add the sliced garlic, the lemon zest, the oregano and half the dill to the pan. Tilt the skillet and spoon the hot, herb-scented oil over the flesh for 60–90 seconds. The salmon is done at 63°C (145°F) at the thickest point — barely opaque at the centre, still glossy, and it will flake with a gentle push. Move the fillets to a warm plate skin-side up so the crust stays crisp.
7. Wipe the skillet if the garlic caught, then return it to medium heat with the remaining 1 tbsp olive oil, the grated garlic and the red pepper flakes. Stir for 30 seconds — fragrant, not browned. Add the beans, the lemon juice, the Dijon, the capers and 2 tbsp water. Simmer for 2–3 minutes, stirring, until the beans are hot through and the liquid has thickened enough to coat them. Off the heat, fold in the parsley and the remaining dill, then taste and add salt.
8. Spoon the warm beans onto four plates, lay the asparagus alongside, and set a salmon fillet on top of each. Pour any juices from the salmon plate over the beans and serve immediately, while the skin is still audible under the fork.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 535 kcal
- Protein: 42 g
- Carbohydrates: 34 g
- Fat: 26 g
- Fiber: 10 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

The dry brine and the dry skin matter more than the marinade here — lemon and herbs go in the pan, not on the fish, so the zest blooms in hot oil instead of scorching under the oven grill. If your asparagus is pencil-thin, cut the roasting time to 6–8 minutes; if it is thumb-thick, add 3 minutes and rotate the tray once. Arctic char, rainbow trout or halibut all work in place of salmon — cook halibut to the same 63°C (145°F) and give it 1 minute less per side. Butter beans or chickpeas swap cleanly for cannellini, and broccolini or green beans stand in for asparagus without changing the timing much.
