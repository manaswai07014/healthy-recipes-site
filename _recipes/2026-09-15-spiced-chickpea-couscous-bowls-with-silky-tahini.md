# Spiced Chickpea & Couscous Bowls with Silky Tahini

There's something deeply satisfying about building a bowl where every element plays a distinct role. This Mediterranean assembly layers fluffy whole wheat couscous beneath spiced chickpeas that shatter into crispy, golden bites—their earthy cumin seasoning cutting through the richness of a silky tahini sauce. Fresh cucumber adds cool crunch while cherry tomatoes burst with sweetness against the earthy grains. The tahini sauce, made with just lemon, garlic, and water to thin the sesame paste into a luxuriously drizzleable consistency, ties everything together with a subtle tang. This is weeknight cooking that feels considered: 25 minutes from start to plate, yet the interplay of temperatures, textures, and flavors suggests hours of thought. Leftover components hold beautifully in the fridge, ready to be reassembled for tomorrow's lunch.

## Ingredients

- 1 can (15 oz) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
- 1 tbsp olive oil, divided
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt, divided
- 3/4 cup whole wheat couscous
- 2 cups loosely packed mixed salad greens
- 1 medium cucumber, diced (about 1 cup)
- 1 cup halved cherry tomatoes
- 3 tbsp tahini
- 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
- 1 small garlic clove, grated
- 3-4 tbsp water
- 1 tbsp chopped fresh parsley

## Instructions

1. Preheat your oven to 400°F (200°C). Spread the drained chickpeas on a clean kitchen towel and pat thoroughly dry—this step is non-negotiable for achieving crispy results. Toss with 1/2 tablespoon olive oil, cumin, and 1/4 teaspoon salt until evenly coated.
2. Spread chickpeas in a single layer on a rimmed baking sheet. Roast for 20-25 minutes, giving the pan a shake halfway through, until edges are golden and blistered. Remove from oven and set aside.
3. While chickpeas roast, bring 1 1/4 cups water to a rolling boil in a medium saucepan with remaining 1/2 tablespoon olive oil and 1/4 teaspoon salt. Add the couscous in a steady stream, stir once, then immediately remove from heat. Cover and let steam for 5 minutes undisturbed.
4. While couscous steams, whisk together tahini, lemon juice, grated garlic, and remaining 1/4 teaspoon salt. Add water tablespoon by tablespoon, whisking constantly, until you reach a pourable but still thick consistency—about the texture of heavy cream. The sauce will thicken as it sits.
5. Fluff the couscous with a fork, then fold in the parsley. Taste and adjust seasoning if needed. The grains should be tender with a slight nuttiness and visible separation.
6. Divide salad greens between two shallow bowls. Create a nest with 1/2 cup couscous in the center of each bowl. Arrange cucumber and tomatoes in distinct sections around the grains. Pile crispy chickpeas on top. Drizzle each bowl with 2-3 tablespoons of tahini sauce and serve immediately.

## Chef's Tips

For the crispiest chickpeas, ensure they're completely dry before roasting and resist the urge to stir them—let them develop deep golden contact points on the pan. If your tahini sauce seizes up when you add lemon juice (this happens when the sesame paste is particularly fresh), simply add more water gradually and whisk vigorously until it smooths out. You can prep components up to 3 days ahead: store roasted chickpeas in a paper bag at room temperature to maintain crispness, keep tahini sauce in a sealed jar, and store vegetables and couscous separately in the fridge. Assemble fresh when ready to serve, as the greens wilt quickly under the warm components.
