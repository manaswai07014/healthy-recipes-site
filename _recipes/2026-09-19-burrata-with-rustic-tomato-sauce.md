# Burrata with Rustic Tomato Sauce

There's a reason Italian grandmothers never put cream in tomato sauce—fresh cheese does the richness work. This dish puts that philosophy into practice: a simple pan of crushed San Marzano tomatoes, fragrant with garlic and brightened by torn basil, serves as the backdrop for burrata's decadent creaminess. The key is technique: cook the garlic just until it's golden and fragrant, not brown and bitter. Let the sauce reduce until it clings to the pasta without pooling in the bowl. When you pierce that burrata at the table, the molten center should cascade across the noodles like a silky sauce of its own. This is the kind of dish that looks effortful but rewards simplicity.

## Ingredients

- 2 oz dry spaghetti
- 2 tbsp extra virgin olive oil, divided
- 3 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 14 oz can whole San Marzano tomatoes
- 1/2 tsp fine sea salt
- 3/4 cup fresh basil, roughly torn
- 1 cup mixed cherry tomatoes, halved
- 1/4 cup Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
- 4.5 oz fresh burrata cheese (1 ball)
- Freshly cracked black pepper

## Instructions

1. Bring a large pot of generously salted water to a rolling boil. Cook the spaghetti until toothsome—pull it 2 minutes before package timing indicates, then drain immediately. Reserve 1/2 cup pasta water before draining.
2. While the pasta cooks, warm 1 tablespoon olive oil in a wide saucepan over medium heat. Add the sliced garlic and watch for the moment it turns pale gold and releases its aroma, about 45 seconds. Pour in the whole tomatoes carefully (the liquid will splatter), then crush them with a wooden spoon into rough, uneven pieces. Season with salt and half the basil.
3. Bring the sauce to an active simmer, partially cover, and cook 10-12 minutes until it thickens and the raw tomato taste fades. The texture should be rustic and chunky—resist the urge to blend it smooth.
4. Toss the drained pasta into the sauce with a splash of pasta water. Agitate the pan to coat every strand, adding more water if the mixture looks dry. Divide between warm shallow bowls.
5. Scatter the halved cherry tomatoes around the edges. Dust with grated Parmigiano. Nestle the burrata in the center, drizzle with remaining olive oil, and finish with cracked pepper, remaining basil, and a pinch of flaky salt. Break the burrata tableside and fold it through the pasta.
6. Serve immediately while the burrata is still molten at the center.

## Chef's Tips

The burrata should be at room temperature for at least 20 minutes before serving—cold cheese won't release its signature cream. If San Marzano tomatoes aren't available, any whole peeled plum tomatoes work, but reduce the salt slightly as brands vary. For a more intense tomato flavor, char the cherry tomatoes in a dry skillet for 3 minutes before adding them to the plate. Leftovers don't freeze well (the burrata texture suffers), but the tomato sauce alone keeps refrigerated for 5 days—simply cook fresh pasta when ready to serve.
