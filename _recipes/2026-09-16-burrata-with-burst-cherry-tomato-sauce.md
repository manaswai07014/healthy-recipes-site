# Burrata with Burst Cherry Tomato Sauce

There's a particular pleasure in a dish that lets exceptional ingredients speak for themselves. This pasta celebrates that philosophy—a loose, chunky tomato sauce built from whole San Marzanos crushed by hand, their bright acidity tempered by fragrant basil and golden garlic. The sauce bubbles and reduces until it coats the spaghetti with concentrated, jammy flavor. Then comes the star: a ball of burrata, its exterior holding firm while the interior cream oozes out when torn. The effect is dramatic yet effortless—rich, flowing cheese binding everything together as it melts into the warm pasta. Cherry tomatoes add sweetness and textural contrast, bursting against the backdrop of the silky sauce. This is the kind of dinner that feels special enough for company yet comes together in well under an hour.

## Ingredients

- 14-oz can crushed San Marzano tomatoes
- 5 oz spaghetti
- 1 cup mixed cherry tomatoes, halved
- 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil, divided
- 3/4 tsp kosher salt, divided
- 1/2 cup torn fresh basil, divided
- 4-oz ball fresh burrata cheese
- 2 tbsp grated Parmesan
- 1/4 tsp freshly cracked black pepper

## Instructions

1. Place a 3-quart saucepan over medium heat. Add 1 tablespoon of the olive oil, then scatter in the sliced garlic. Let the garlic sizzle gently for 45 to 60 seconds, watching closely until it turns pale gold and releases its aroma. Pour in the crushed tomatoes with their liquid, being cautious of any splattering. Stir in 1/2 teaspoon of the salt, then increase heat to medium-high until the sauce reaches a vigorous simmer. Maintain this rapid bubble for 10 to 12 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the sauce reduces by roughly one-third and thickens noticeably.
2. While the sauce reduces, bring a large pot of water to a rolling boil. Season the water generously—it should taste distinctly salty, like a mild broth. Add the spaghetti and cook until just tender, approximately 1 minute less than the package directions indicate. The pasta should hold its shape with a slight bite at the center. Reserve 1/2 cup of the starchy cooking water before draining.
3. Stir the halved cherry tomatoes into the simmering sauce. Cook for 2 minutes until they begin to soften and release their juices. Add half the torn basil and stir to incorporate. Taste and adjust seasoning with remaining salt.
4. Add the drained pasta to the sauce. Toss vigorously, adding splashes of reserved pasta water to create a light coating that clings to each strand. Divide between two warmed shallow bowls.
5. Nestle half the burrata ball onto each portion. Scatter the grated Parmesan over the top, then finish with the remaining basil, a drizzle of the remaining olive oil, and a crack of fresh black pepper. Serve immediately, instructing guests to tear the burrata and toss it through the pasta at the table.

## Chef's Tips

For the best burrata experience, pull it from the refrigerator 15 minutes before serving—the cheese should be room temperature to fully release its creamy center. If you can't find burrata, fresh mozzarella torn into pieces makes a fine substitute, though you'll lose that signature ooze. The sauce can be made up to three days ahead and refrigerated; reheat gently and add the cherry tomatoes fresh when assembling. Any pasta shape works here—bucatini catches the sauce beautifully in its hollow center, while penne or rigatoni would provide more pockets for the chunky tomato bits. Leftovers will keep for two days, though the burrata is best added fresh to each serving rather than stored.
