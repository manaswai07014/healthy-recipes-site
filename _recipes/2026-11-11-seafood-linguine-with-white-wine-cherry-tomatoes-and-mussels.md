# Seafood Linguine with White Wine, Cherry Tomatoes and Mussels

Shrimp shells simmered in the wine build a briny backbone cream can't match — garlic sizzles, mussels hiss open, sauce clings to whole-wheat linguine.

## Ingredients

(Makes 4 servings)

- 240 g (8.5 oz) dry whole-wheat linguine
- 400 g (14 oz) raw shrimp, peeled and deveined
- 600 g (1.3 lb) mussels in shell, scrubbed and debearded
- 300 g (10 oz) cherry tomatoes, halved
- 4 garlic cloves, sliced paper-thin
- 1/2 tsp red chili flakes
- 240 ml (1 cup) dry white wine
- 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
- 1 tbsp tomato paste
- 1 lemon (zest + 1 tbsp juice)
- 20 g (1 small bunch) flat-leaf parsley, chopped
- 1/2 tsp sea salt, plus more for the pasta water
- 1/4 tsp freshly ground black pepper

## Method

1. Bring 4 litres of water to a rolling boil in a wide pot and salt it until it tastes like the sea (about 2 tbsp). Keep it at a hard, moving boil — whole-wheat linguine needs aggressive water or the strands glue together.
2. Peel the shrimp, dropping the shells into a small saucepan; refrigerate the peeled shrimp. Add the white wine and 120 ml water to the shells, bring to a bare simmer and cook 5 minutes. The liquid should smell sweet and briny, not sharp with raw alcohol. Strain through a fine sieve, pressing the shells with a spoon, and discard them. You should have roughly 250 ml of coral-tinted stock.
3. Scrub the mussels under cold running water, pull off any beards, and tap each one on the counter — a live mussel snaps shut. Discard any that stay open. Halve the cherry tomatoes, slice the garlic paper-thin, and chop the parsley.
4. Warm the olive oil in a large, deep skillet over medium-low heat. Add the garlic and chili flakes and cook 60-90 seconds, stirring, until the garlic turns pale gold and smells nutty. If it starts to brown, pull the pan off the heat for 30 seconds — browned garlic turns the whole sauce bitter. Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1 minute, until it darkens to brick red and smells sweet rather than tinny.
5. Add the cherry tomatoes and a pinch of salt. Raise the heat to medium and cook 4-5 minutes, pressing them with the back of a spoon until they collapse and their skins wrinkle. The pan should look glossy and jammy, with the oil separating slightly at the edges.
6. Pour in the shrimp-shell stock and simmer 3 minutes, until reduced by about half and the sauce coats the back of a spoon.
7. Drop the linguine into the boiling water and cook 1 minute less than the packet says — it should still have a chalky core when you bite it.
8. Meanwhile, tip the mussels into the skillet, cover, and steam 3-4 minutes over medium heat, shaking the pan once. They will hiss and pop open. Lift them out with a slotted spoon into a bowl and discard any that remain shut. Cover the bowl loosely to keep them warm.
9. Add the peeled shrimp to the sauce in a single layer and cook 2 minutes per side, just until they curl into loose C shapes and turn opaque pink — 63°C/145°F at the centre. Pull them the moment they are done; a tight O shape means they have gone rubbery.
10. Reserve 250 ml of the pasta water, then drain the linguine. Add the pasta to the skillet with 120 ml of the pasta water and toss hard over medium heat for 1-2 minutes, until the sauce turns creamy and clings to every strand. Add more pasta water a splash at a time if it looks dry — the starch is what emulsifies the oil and wine into a sauce.
11. Return the mussels to the pan, then add the parsley, the lemon zest and 1 tbsp lemon juice. Toss once, grind over the black pepper, taste for salt, and divide between four warmed bowls, spooning the pan juices over the top.

## Nutrition (per serving)

- Calories: 535 kcal
- Protein: 43 g
- Carbohydrates: 52 g
- Fat: 12 g
- Fiber: 6 g

## 👨‍🍳 Chef Tips

The shell stock is the whole trick here: 5 minutes of simmering shrimp shells in wine gives you the deep, saline sweetness that cream normally provides, at a fraction of the calories. If you buy shell-on shrimp you get this for free; if yours are already peeled, simmer 2 tbsp of the wine with a pinch of saffron or a 2-inch strip of orange zest instead. Clams, squid rings or a frozen frutti di mare mix all work in place of the mussels — add squid in the last 90 seconds only, or it turns to rubber. No wine in the house? Use 120 ml dry vermouth, or 120 ml stock plus 1 tbsp white wine vinegar added off the heat. For gluten-free pasta, cook it separately and sauce it immediately; it will not emulsify as generously, so add an extra splash of pasta water. This dish is best eaten the day it is made — the mussels and shrimp toughen on reheating. If you must, keep the sauce and seafood separate from the pasta for up to 2 days, then reheat gently in a covered pan with a spoonful of water.
