instructions:
  - "Warm olive oil in a heavy-bottomed pot over medium heat until it shimmers slightly, about 1 minute. Add sliced leek with a pinch of salt and cook, stirring occasionally, until softened and translucent, 4-5 minutes. The leeks should smell sweet and mild, not browned."
  - "Add diced carrots and a splash of water (about 2 tablespoons). Cover and cook until carrots begin to soften, 3-4 minutes. Uncover, add red pepper cubes, and cook 2 minutes more until vegetables are just tender but still holding their shape."
  - "Stir in tomato paste and cook for 1 minute, coating the vegetables evenly. The paste will darken slightly and smell deeply savory—this is the foundation of the broth."
  - "Pour in white wine and let it bubble vigorously for 30 seconds to cook off the alcohol. Add saffron threads, stock, and bay leaf. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a steady simmer."
  - "Add potato cubes if using, or proceed if omitting. Simmer uncovered for 8 minutes until vegetables are completely tender and the broth takes on a pale golden hue from the saffron."
  - "Season the broth generously with salt and pepper. Nestle white fish pieces into the simmering liquid along with salmon chunks. Cook undisturbed for exactly 3 minutes—the fish will turn opaque and flake at the edges when done. Resist stirring to keep the pieces intact."
  - "Remove from heat. Stir in heavy cream, lemon zest, and half the lemon juice. Taste and adjust seasoning. The broth should be glossy and fragrant with a balanced citrus brightness."
  - "Ladle into warmed bowls. Scatter fresh dill over the top and serve immediately with crusty bread and the remaining lemon wedges on the side."
introduction: |
  There is something deeply comforting about a well-made fish soup—the way the saffron-tinted broth turns silky as the cream melds with the briny stock, how the fish practically dissolves into tender, flaky pockets. This Mediterranean version layers flavor carefully: sweet leeks and carrots form the aromatic base, tomato paste adds depth without overwhelming, and a careful simmer coaxes the fish into submission without turning it tough or rubbery. The salmon contributes richness while the white fish stays pristine. A final flourish of lemon zest and fresh dill lifts the entire bowl, making this soup feel both nourishing and special enough for a quiet dinner for two on a weeknight.
chef_tips: |
  For the cleanest flavor, use a neutral fish stock or make your own from scraps. If using bottled stock, taste it first—some brands are quite salty, which will affect your seasoning. The saffron is optional but adds a subtle honey-floral note and beautiful golden color; a pinch goes a long way. For a dairy-free version, swap the cream for a tablespoon of olive oil stirred in at the end—the soup will be slightly less glossy but equally flavorful. Leftovers keep well for 2 days in the refrigerator; the fish will continue to absorb broth, so the soup thickens considerably. Reheat gently over medium-low heat, adding a splash of water or stock to loosen if needed.
